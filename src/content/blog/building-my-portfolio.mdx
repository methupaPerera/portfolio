# Building My First Developer Portfolio

When I decided to build my first developer portfolio, I thought it would be easy.

It wasn’t.

It forced me to think about design, performance, accessibility, and how I present myself online. This post documents what I learned and what I would do differently next time.

---

## Why I Built It

As a student and web developer, I needed a place to:

- Showcase my projects
- Practice modern frameworks
- Learn deployment and optimization
- Experiment with UI ideas

A portfolio is more than a website. It’s a long-term learning project.

---

## Tech Stack

For this project, I used:

- **Next.js** for routing and performance
- **TypeScript** for safety
- **Tailwind CSS** for styling
- **MDX** for content

This stack gave me flexibility without slowing me down.

---

## Biggest Challenges

### 1. Design Decisions

Choosing colors, layouts, and typography took more time than coding.

Perfectionism is expensive.

### 2. Performance

Optimizing images and fonts mattered more than I expected.  
A fast site feels professional. A slow one feels unfinished.

### 3. Content Writing

Writing about yourself is harder than writing code.

No compiler errors. Just self-doubt.

---


---

## What I Learned

Building this site taught me:

- How to structure a real project
- How to debug deployment issues
- How to write for humans
- How to finish things

Finishing is underrated.

---

## What’s Next

Next, I plan to:

- Add more case studies
- Improve accessibility
- Write deeper technical posts
- Keep shipping

Progress beats perfection.

---

## Final Thoughts

If you’re waiting until you’re “ready” to build your site, stop.

You get ready by building.

Start small. Improve often. Repeat.
